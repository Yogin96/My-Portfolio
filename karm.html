Simplifying attendance, leave & payroll for an unconventional workplace

Devalok (the design studio I work for) operates on a unique break framework, where each associate is assigned 122 days of leave per year to use at their convenience without fixed weekends or predefined holidays. This made traditional attendance systems unsuitable, as no existing tool supported such a flexible leave structure.

Hence Karm was created

It started as a simple MVP built by developers to manage attendance and breaks. Initially, it worked. But as we began using it daily as a team, several usability issues started to surface. Here are key screens from the MVP.

Users Insights

These insights helped us identify the core usability gaps in the system

These insights helped us identify the core usability gaps in the system

  • Associates often forgot to mark attendance

  • Leave balances were not clearly visible

  • Attendance records and leave history were difficult to track

  • Payroll and attendance information lacked clarity

  • It was difficult to identify missed attendance or absences

  • Admin had no notification on break requests

The team size was 4-5 members while we were using it, these seemed like small issues. But as the team grew, they would compound making the system harder to rely on for both employees and admins.

This led to the decision to redesign Karm from the ground up—focusing on improving visibility, reducing friction, and building a system that could scale reliably with the team.

Improving attendance, leave tracking, and payroll visibility

Marking attendance was one of the most frequent actions in the app, yet it was often missed. Users had to remember to mark attendance manually, and there was no strong visual cue or reminder to prompt this action. This led to inconsistent records and required manual corrections later.

Key Design Decisions

  1. Make attendance action highly visible

To reduce missed entries, the attendance CTA was placed prominently on the home screen, ensuring it is the first action users notice.

  1. Provide real-time status feedback

Users can instantly see whether attendance is marked, not marked, or on break—removing ambiguity.

3. Introduce reminders and constraints

The system nudges users to complete required actions (like adding a task before marking attendance), reducing incomplete or incorrect entries. Sending an email reminder once in few hours.

4. Use calendar views for better tracking

Weekly and monthly views allow users to easily track past attendance and identify gaps.

Admin

Enabling quick visibility and faster decision-making

For admin, tracking team attendance and managing requests was time-consuming and unclear.

Key Design Decisions

  1. Surface key attendance metrics upfront

The dashboard highlights who is present, absent, not marked, or on break—allowing admins to quickly assess team status without navigating deeper.

  1. Enable quick action on incoming requests

Break and attendance requests are surfaced prominently, enabling admins to review and act on them without switching contexts.

3. Introduce filters for better control

Admins can filter by associate to quickly access relevant data, reducing time spent searching through records.
