Redesigning how people discover live performances & events

Sabhalog is a philanthropic platform connecting artists, organizers, and attendees across India and the US diaspora. It started with Indian performing arts but expanded to include genres like standup comedy — built to serve any live performance community, not just one.

When I started, the MVP was live but rough. No consistent design language, outdated UI, weak branding, and flows that didn't account for three very different user types.

Understanding the problem

Audit, stakeholder interviews, and competitive analysis pointed to four core gaps:

  • Onboarding locked users into a single permanent role

  • Series cards cluttered with numbers that gave no sense of progress

  • No clean path to add offline or unregistered artists to events

  • No visual identity that reflected the world the app served

Onboarding & Role Architecture

Onboarding & Role Architecture

The original onboarding was a one-way door.

Open app → Enter email → Enter OTP → Choose role (Attendee / Artist / Org) → Enter profile details → Done. Role locked

A musician is also an audience member. An organizer might also perform. The app forced real people into permanent boxes with no way out. It also asked for email before giving users any reason to trust the product.

Key design decisions

Everyone enters as an attendee

  • Zero friction entry. Discover events, explore the community, experience value first. No commitment at the door.

Roles are additive, not permanent

  • Artist and Organizer are added from the Profile page, when the user is ready. A user can be Attendee only, Attendee + Artist, Attendee + Org, or all three.

Only introducing Sabhalog in the Onboarding

  • Three screens introduce what Sabhalog offers each user type and take the user directly to the Explore page — value first, login later.

Key design decisions

Key design decisions

Accordion over stepper

I initially explored a numbered step indicator (Details → Dates → Slots → Review), a standard linear wizard. It was rejected in favour of an accordion pattern for two reasons:

  • Consistency. The app already uses accordion sections for Artist and Org application forms. Reusing the pattern reduces cognitive load across the product.

  • Navigation logic. With a traditional stepper, going back risks losing context or retriggering validation. With the accordion, completed sections stay locked and visible. Going back is just reopening a section. No back button, no lost work.

Template over per-day configuration

  • Define the typical event pattern once — e.g. 2 events per day at 6pm and 8pm — applied across all dates. Exceptions handled later in the Series Planner. Optimize for the common case. Let editing handle the exceptions.

Add Event — Artist Search & Add

Not all artists are on the app. Older performers rely on calls and in-person confirmation. The flow had to handle both searchable digital profiles and manually entered artists who may never log in.

Also, an artist might list Vocal, Violin, and Tabla on their profile — but only perform Vocal at a specific event. Organizers needed to specify skills per event, not just per artist.

Three iterations to get it right

Iteration 1. Inline expansion

Artist search lived inside the form step. Selecting an artist expanded an inline card with genre chips. Simple, but when "Add Artist" for offline artists was introduced, an inline card couldn't carry both intents. The offline path needed more fields (name, photo, genre, email) that broke the pattern.

Iteration 2. Search-first popup

Artist management moved into a dedicated popup: search first, and if not found, add them. Cleaner, but adding an offline artist triggered another popup on top of the first. A popup spawning a popup signals escalating complexity at exactly the moment users need simplicity.

Final solution. Two parallel entry points

The split is surfaced upfront inside the form itself:

  • Search Artist opens the search popup (recent collaborations first, genre filtering, custom skill tags)

  • Add Artist Details opens a separate add popup (name, photo, genre, email)

No nesting. No ambiguity. Two distinct intents, two purpose-built flows.

Branding

The MVP had a generic blue, orange, and white palette. Functional, but culturally neutral. It could have been any SaaS product.

Even as the platform expanded its scope, its roots and the bulk of its community are in Indian performing arts. The visual identity needed to reflect that cultural depth while staying open enough to not feel exclusive.

I built the brand from scratch, starting with a moodboard drawn from that world — the warm glow of a sabha hall, aged manuscripts, traditional instruments, textiles. The moodboard kept pointing to the same palette — the terracotta of temple walls, the deep reds of sarees, the warm browns of traditional instruments. Muted Vermillion wasn't chosen, it emerged

Moodboard of images & colors

From this we decided we would be having only 2 colors.

  • Muted Vermilion as Primary as its has an Indian vibe to it & most of the instruments are in this color.

  • Warm White as secondary base color for surfaces. This is goes well with the primary color.

Typography we went for

  • Libre Baskerville for the primary typeface. A screen-optimised serif with classical editorial weight. Signals heritage and culture without feeling archaic.

  • Noto Sans is the secondary typeface. Clean, highly legible, and designed to support virtually all languages and scripts including Indian scripts. A deliberate choice for an app serving both English-dominant diaspora users and India-based users.
